# Map competitive whitespace

## Purpose

Determine whether the team’s proposed mechanism, target context, proof, or operating model has a defensible remaining gap—and adapt the strategy when it does not. A whitespace finding is a decision aid, not proof that no competitor, patent, or prior entry exists.

## Search four distinct landscapes

Do not collapse unlike evidence into one “competitor” list.

| Landscape | What to inspect | Question answered |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Current services | live products, government programs, internal/public workflows, substitutes | What can a user already do today? |
| Technical prior art | papers, repositories, standards, public technical reports | What mechanism is already known? |
| Competition precedents | official past entries, winners, program showcases | What has already been proposed in a similar judging context? |
| Patent / rights signals | official patent databases and rights notices when relevant | Is there a visible rights or licensing concern to investigate? |

Log queries, databases, dates, geographic/language scope, access limits, and result status. Use primary or official sources for material conclusions; label secondary summaries as leads.

## Compare mechanisms, not slogans

For each close alternative, map:

```text
ALTERNATIVE_MAP
- source and status (implemented | research | proposal | patent record):
- target user and decision moment:
- input / data dependency:
- mechanism and human role:
- outcome claimed and evidence quality:
- operating constraints:
- overlap with our proposal:
- meaningful difference still requiring proof:
```

Position the idea only on differences that change a user decision, cost, risk, adoption constraint, or measurable proof. A different UI, wording, or model name alone is not meaningful whitespace.

## Make an explicit strategy decision

Choose one per close alternative:

- **differentiate** — a decision-relevant gap remains and has a proof plan;
- **narrow** — preserve the problem but reduce to a neglected context or mechanism;
- **integrate / acknowledge** — existing work solves part of the problem and should be cited or used as a dependency;
- **pivot** — the claimed advantage is gone or cannot be evidenced;
- **escalate** — rights, licensing, or legal questions need qualified review.

For patents, describe only a preliminary public-record screen. Do not claim non-infringement, patentability, or freedom to operate; recommend qualified counsel for decisions that require them.

## Output

Return a search register, comparison matrix, overlap map, residual-whitespace claims with required proof, and `DIFFERENTIATION_LOCK` containing selected mechanism, non-claims, excluded features, risks, and reopen triggers. Use “not confirmed within the stated search scope” for negative results.
